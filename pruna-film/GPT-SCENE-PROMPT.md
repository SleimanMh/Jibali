You are the art director and still photographer for a 20-second vertical (9:16) brand film for JIBALI (جبالي), a whole-wheat Lebanese saj bread from Rashaya, at the foot of Mount Hermon (Jabal al-Sheikh / Haramoun). The bread is made from only: 100% whole-wheat flour, water, natural leaven (sourdough) and salt — baked over a wood fire on a saj. Story: the hoopoe (هدهد) carries the story from the mountain to the home kitchen: wheat of our grandparents → stone mill → water, salt and natural leaven → hands → fire → bread.

YOUR TASK
For EACH scene below, (1) generate ONE photorealistic vertical image, 9:16 (1080×1920), and (2) write a 10-second image-to-video motion prompt for that image (for MiniMax Hailuo or Wan 2.2). Work one scene at a time, in order.

REALISM RULES — apply to every image (most important)
- It must look like a REAL photograph by a documentary photographer, not AI art and not a glossy food advert.
- Natural, imperfect light: daylight from a doorway or window, real shadows, no glow, no HDR, no teal-and-orange grade, no oversaturation.
- Real textures and wear: flour dust, cracks in stone, scratches on wood, worn cloth, slightly uneven bread, real dirt and imperfection.
- Ordinary real people: an elderly Lebanese village woman with wrinkled, sun-worn hands, a simple dark dress or cardigan; show hands and forearms, avoid showing the face. Same woman, same clothes, same kitchen in every scene with people.
- Correct anatomy: five fingers on each hand, natural nails, natural skin.
- Real camera feel: 35mm or a good phone camera, modest depth of field, slight grain, eye-level or natural angles.
- Authentic Lebanese village setting: limestone walls, old wooden table, copper and clay pots, linen cloth with simple red/blue stripes.
- NO text, letters, logos, labels, watermarks or packaging in any image.

SCENES
1. JABAL AL-SHEIKH AT SUNRISE (0–2 s)
   The long, broad, gently rounded snow-capped ridge of Mount Hermon (a wide massif with soft summits — NOT a pointed volcano-like peak), first sunlight from the right, morning mist in the valley, the village of Rashaya with stone houses and red-tiled roofs on the hillside below. Shot from a hillside like a real landscape photo.
   Motion: clouds and mist drift slowly through the valley, light warms gradually, very slow push-in.

2. THE HOOPOE (2–4 s)
   A real Eurasian hoopoe (Upupa epops): orange-buff body, bold black-and-white barred wings and tail, long thin down-curved bill, black-tipped crest. In flight above the valley near Rashaya, side view, wings open mid-beat, mountain softly out of focus behind. Like a real wildlife photo (fast shutter, natural light).
   Motion: the bird flies left to right with natural undulating wingbeats; the camera follows gently.

3. THE WHEAT OF OUR GRANDPARENTS (4–6 s)
   A field of ripe, slightly uneven, old-variety mountain wheat on a terraced slope with stone walls, Hermon behind. Close on the ears, with an old farmer's hand gently running through them. Late-afternoon light, dust in the air, some bent stalks.
   Motion: wheat sways in a light breeze, the hand brushes the ears, very slow push.

4. THE STONE MILL (6–8 s)
   How it is done: cleaned whole-wheat kernels are poured from a wooden or copper bowl into a traditional stone mill — two round flat millstones stacked horizontally (the top one turns), not an upright wheel. Coarse, light-brown whole-wheat flour with bran specks comes out from the edge into a wooden trough. Dusty old mill room, light through a small window.
   Motion: kernels pour steadily, the top stone turns slowly, flour trickles out, dust drifts in the light beam.

5. FLOUR, WATER, SALT AND NATURAL LEAVEN (8–10 s)
   How it is done: whole-wheat flour heaped in a wide wooden bowl with a well in the centre; the woman's hand sprinkles coarse salt, water is poured from a clay jug, a spoonful of natural sourdough leaven (a small bowl of bubbly beige starter) beside the bowl.
   Motion: salt falls from the fingertips, a thin stream of water pours into the well, the flour darkens where it lands.

6. THE HANDS (10–12 s)
   How it is done: the woman kneads the light-brown whole-wheat dough in the wooden bowl or on the floured table, pushing with the heels of her palms, folding and turning; flour on her fingers and wrists.
   Motion: slow, steady kneading — press, fold, turn; the dough stays one piece; real-time speed.

7. STRETCHING THE SAJ DOUGH (12–13 s)
   How it is done in Lebanon: a small ball of dough is flattened, then stretched paper-thin by tossing and turning it between the hands and forearms, then laid on a round padded cushion (الكارة, "kara") to carry it to the saj.
   Motion: the thin dough sheet is turned and stretched over the backs of the hands, slowly, catching the light.

8. THE FIRE (13–15 s)
   How it is done: a convex steel saj dome (like an upside-down wok) resting on stones over a small wood fire of olive or oak branches, outdoors under an old stone arch or in a village yard.
   Motion: flames flicker under the dome, small sparks and thin smoke rise; still camera.

9. THE BREAD ON THE SAJ (15–17 s)
   How it is done: the paper-thin dough is flipped from the kara onto the hot dome; within about a minute small bubbles rise and turn golden-brown; she flips it once with her fingertips. The bread stays paper-thin and drapes over the dome.
   Motion: bubbles rise and brown, faint steam, she lifts and turns the bread.

10. THE BREAD AT HOME (17–19 s)
    Freshly baked saj folded in quarters, stacked on a striped linen cloth in a basket or on the wooden table, warm, slightly irregular, with natural brown spots.
    Motion: soft steam rises, a hand takes one folded bread.

11. FINAL BACKGROUND PLATE (19–20 s), no product
    An empty corner of the same wooden table with a folded linen cloth and soft window light, with clean space in the centre. Do NOT draw any package, bag, label or logo; the real JIBALI package photo will be placed here later.
